ABSTRACT This study advances our previous research on radio frequency (RF) source localization using
unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) through a novel geometrical Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI)
approach. Our methodology utilizes four UAVs, split into two groups, which adjust their positions until
the power received by each UAV matches that of its counterpart, allowing the calculation of intersecting
lines that pinpoint the source location. Recognizing the challenges posed by environmental noise which
can alter signal values leading to inaccurate estimations, we introduce a smoother integral sliding mode
filter. This enhanced filter incorporates the integral of the error into the sliding mode control framework to
address the high-frequency chattering typically associated with sliding mode filters in noisy environments.
This adaptation not only reduces the chattering effect but also stabilizes the UAV’s response to fluctuating
signal strengths, resulting in more accurate and reliable localization. Simulation results demonstrate a marked
improvement in localization accuracy and robustness against noise.

in UAV-based RF source localization, this study introduces
a background that incorporates sliding mode filters into the
localization process. The sliding mode filter, known for its
robustness against disturbances and uncertainties, is applied
to the RSSI data to produce more accurate distance estimations, thereby improving the localization accuracy of the RF
source.

Sliding Mode Filter (SMF) is an advanced estimation technique that applies the principles of sliding mode control to the
problem of filtering and state estimation in dynamic systems.
At its core, SMF aims to robustly estimate the states of a
system by driving the estimation errors towards a predefined
sliding surface and ensuring they remain on this surface
despite disturbances or model uncertainties [14].
The performance of the sliding mode filter is influenced
by several critical parameters, including the design of the
sliding surface, the gain matrix, the coefficient of any integral
terms, and the characteristics of system noise. The sliding
surface determines the robustness and convergence speed of
the system, while the gain matrix controls the response to
deviations from this surface, where improper tuning can lead
to chattering or instability [14]. Noise characteristics, such
as amplitude and frequency, also play a significant role in
the filter’s effectiveness, as does the system’s sampling rate,
which impacts the filter’s ability to respond to changes and
suppress noise. The sensitivity of the filter to these parameters
requires careful tuning to optimize performance, particularly
in dynamic environments where real-time adaptation may
be necessary. Recent advancements in sliding mode control,
including adaptive techniques, offer promising approaches
to dynamically adjust these parameters for enhanced performance in varying conditions [15].

The sliding surface S(x) is chosen such that the system’s
behaviour is satisfactory when the system’s trajectory lies on
this surface. It is typically a function of the estimation error
e = x − x̂, where x̂ is the estimated state. The design of S(x)
aims to ensure that the error dynamics are stable and converge
to zero, i.e., S(x) = 0. A common choice for S(x) in sliding
mode filters is

III. METHODOLOGY

The methodology is detailed in subsequent subsections. Initially, the precise 3D Cartesian positions for each UAV are
determined to enable their movement along two orthogonal
circles—one in the x-y plane and another in the x-z plane,
each at a radius R from the center. Then, the angles of
rotation necessary for the UAVs to orbit the circles while
preserving a separation of 2R between them are computed.
These rotational angles are derived based on the variances in
the power of the signals received. These angles are then used
to calculate 3 orthogonal vectors for target localization.
While UAVs offer significant advantages in a wide range
of applications, their onboard processors typically lack the
capacity for handling computationally intensive tasks, and
efficient energy use is crucial for maintaining their mobility and operational endurance. To address these limitations,
our method offloads the computational burden by having
UAVs transmit the received signal strength data to a more
powerful central computer. This central system then calculates the optimal positions for the UAVs by analysing the
differences in signal strength within the groups, iteratively
updating and transmitting new position commands to the
UAVs until they achieve equal signal strength. This approach
not only conserves the UAVs’ processing power and energy
but also ensures precise and synchronized adjustments to their
positions, enhancing the overall accuracy and reliability of the
localization process which is summarized in Figure 1.

Chattering in sliding mode control (and by extension, sliding mode filtering) occurs due to the inherent discontinuous
nature of the control law applied to ensure the system’s
states reach and stay on the predefined sliding surface. This
discontinuity arises from the use of a sign function or a similar
discontinuous function in the control law, which leads to rapid
switching of the control input.
In order to mitigate this effect, smoother integral sliding
mode filter is developed in this study. The error integral is
incorporated into the algorithm of the traditional sliding mode
filter to substantially mitigate chattering. This key modification smoothens the control actions, which not only reduces
noise but also enhances the system’s durability. By integrating
the error, the filter achieves improved stability and robustness,
enabling consistent performance even in the face of system
uncertainties and external disturbances. The integral component of the error also significantly improves tracking accuracy
and promotes faster error convergence, which is particularly
valuable in applications such as target tracking with RSSI.

V. CONCLUSION

This paper has presented an advanced method for RF source
localization using UAVs, employing a smoother integral sliding mode filter to enhance the accuracy and reliability of
172417

N. Güzey: Smoother Integral Sliding Mode Filter for RSSI-Based Localization

RSSI-based localization techniques. Through the integration
of the error integral into the sliding mode control framework,
we have successfully mitigated the common issues of chattering and noise sensitivity, which are prevalent in conventional
sliding mode filters.
Our simulations demonstrate a significant improvement in
localization accuracy and robustness against environmental
noise, underscoring the effectiveness of the smoother integral
sliding mode filter. This approach not only stabilizes the
signal fluctuations but also ensures more consistent UAV
positioning, which is crucial for accurate source detection
and localization in complex, real-world environments.
This study primarily focuses on the application of the
smoother integral sliding mode filter under Gaussian noise
conditions, commonly encountered in UAV operations. However, future work will expand on these tests by incorporating different noise structures and additional environmental
factors, such as multipath interference and atmospheric conditions. These extended studies aim to further enhance the
robustness of our localization method under real-world scenarios.
To further enhance the filter’s performance in highly
dynamic environments, future work can incorporate adaptive
or learning-based approaches. These methods will enable
the filter to dynamically adjust its parameters in real-time,
ensuring robust performance even as signal characteristics
rapidly change.
While the proposed method demonstrates significant
potential, there are certain considerations to keep in mind.
The requirement for a minimum of four UAVs, may present
challenges in scenarios where resources are constrained.
Additionally, the method assumes reliable communication
between UAVs, which could face occasional disruptions
due to environmental factors such as signal interference or
obstacles. Although the smoother integral sliding mode filter
effectively reduces the impact of environmental noise, the
method’s performance might still be influenced by extreme
noise conditions or highly complex environments, such as
dense urban areas. These limitations may create new study
topics in the future.
This study contributes to the field of UAV-based localization by providing a robust, practical solution that can be
deployed in GPS-denied environments, potentially benefiting
a wide range of applications from military operations to
emergency response.
